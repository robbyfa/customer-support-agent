# Escalation Policy

## Overview

This policy defines when and how support cases should be escalated to specialist teams. Proper escalation ensures that complex, sensitive, or high-risk issues are handled by the right people within the right timeframe.

## Escalation Tiers

### Tier 1 — Front-line Support
- Handles: general inquiries, simple account questions, FAQ-level issues.
- Resolution target: within the same interaction.

### Tier 2 — Specialist Teams
- **Payments Operations**: failed or delayed withdrawals, missing deposits, duplicate charges, payment provider issues.
- **Promotions Team**: bonus not credited, wagering disputes, manual bonus credits.
- **Technical Team**: login issues not resolved by standard troubleshooting, platform bugs, email delivery failures.
- **Compliance Team**: document verification overdue, account restrictions, regulatory inquiries.
- **Security Team**: suspicious login activity, account compromise, 2FA reset requests.
- **Responsible Gaming Team**: self-exclusion requests, deposit limit changes, at-risk behaviour concerns.

### Tier 3 — Management
- Customer complaints unresolved after two Tier 2 interactions.
- Formal complaints or regulatory body referrals.
- Cases involving potential legal liability.

## Escalation Criteria

A case **must** be escalated when:
- The issue cannot be resolved at the current tier within the expected timeframe.
- The customer explicitly requests to speak to a supervisor or specialist.
- The case involves sensitive matters (responsible gaming, fraud, account compromise).
- The customer has contacted support three or more times about the same issue.
- The case involves a high-value customer (VIP) with an unresolved payment issue.
- There is a risk of regulatory non-compliance.

## Escalation Process

1. Document the full case history, including all previous interactions and attempted resolutions.
2. Classify the escalation reason (technical, payment, compliance, responsible gaming, complaint).
3. Assign the case to the appropriate specialist team.
4. Set the priority level: **low** (informational), **medium** (action within 24 hours), **high** (action within 4 hours), **critical** (immediate action required).
5. Notify the customer that their case has been escalated and provide an expected response time.

## Priority Levels

| Priority | Response Time | Examples |
|----------|--------------|---------|
| Critical | Immediate | Self-exclusion, account compromise, suspected fraud |
| High | Within 4 hours | Failed withdrawals (3+ attempts), compliance hold, VIP issues |
| Medium | Within 24 hours | Missing deposits, bonus disputes, verification delays |
| Low | Within 48 hours | General feedback, feature requests, non-urgent inquiries |

## Anti-Escalation (De-escalation)

- If a customer is upset but the issue can be resolved at the current tier, try to de-escalate first.
- Acknowledge the customer's frustration, summarize the issue, and provide a clear resolution path.
- Only escalate if the issue genuinely requires specialist intervention.

## Escalation Audit

- All escalations are tracked in the audit trail for quality assurance.
- Escalation patterns are reviewed monthly to identify recurring issues and training opportunities.
